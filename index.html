Hej, I'm Nele

I’m interested in making medical care more intelligent, accessible, and humane through machine learning. Over time, my focus has expanded beyond accuracy alone – toward understanding how we can design machine learning models that earn and preserve human trust. As a researcher and PhD student at DFKI, I contribute to the AnoMed project, where I work at the intersection of fundamental research on differential privacy and real medical challenges.

 

My current research explores the intersection of machine learning, differential privacy, and representation learning:

Privacy-friendly feature extraction

Analysing latent space properties and designing feature extraction methods compatible with differentially-private downstream learners.

Differentially-private data synthesis

Generating synthetic datasets with formal privacy guarantees.

Weakly supervised learning

Frameworks that utilize limited or noisy labels, e.g. Multiple Instance Learning.

Feel free to reach out!

Year: 2026

Type: Master Thesis

Description: Investigates whether the morphological difference between wild-type and CLN7-knockout fibroblasts, a rare inherited neurodegenerative disease with a poorly characterised cellular phenotype, can be represented as an editable transformation in label-free microscopy images. A conditional StyleGAN2-ADA generator is trained to synthesise realistic cell images, and a linear SVM in its latent space identifies a wild-type/knockout direction along which counterfactual traversals can be produced; real micrographs are mapped into the same space via W+ projection to enable editing of real cells. The traversal shifts an independent classifier's knockout probability substantially for both synthetic and real cells, and reveals four interpretable morphological features as candidates distinguishing the two cell types.

Additional Information: This work was supported by the Friedrich Wingert scholarship

Year: 2025

Type: Internship

Description: This work investigated machine learning methods to disentangle facial expression from identity-specific appearance in face images. It combined a Feature-Style Encoder for high-quality StyleGAN inversion with contrastive learning to separate emotion-related from identity-related features. As identity-independent augmentations, Style-Mixing and Latent Space Slider were applied

Additional Information: Work was accepted and presented at the annual student conference in Luebeck, Accepted for post-publication: Sudent Conference Paper

Year: 2025

Type: Master Thesis

Description: Deep learning models for medical image segmentation typically rely on costly, expert-annotated pixel-level labels, motivating methods that can learn from weak image-level labels instead. In this work, three in-model explainability methods (Grad-CAM, CLAM, and PIP-Net) were compared for detecting lung diseases in chest X-rays. It was evaluated how well each recovers pixel-level, spatial insight from image-level supervision alone.

Year: 2024

Type: Internship

Description: Investigates whether Vision Transformers are vulnerable to Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs), which try to determine whether a given example was part of a model's training data. Analyses the performance of LiRA, a state-of-the-art MIA based on per-example difficulty scores and a Gaussian likelihood-ratio test, on ViTs, and adapts it to additionally exploit information from the models' self-attention maps.

Year: 2024

Type: Internship

Description: Proof-of-concept for detecting pathological lymph nodes in CT scans using multiple-instance learning, which avoids the need for pixel-level labels by learning from image-level labels alone. A CLAM-based model was trained on synthetic data designed to imitate lymph node-like shapes, and the effects of patch size, top-k values, and patch count were examined.

Additional Information: Work was accepted and presented at the annual student conference in Luebeck.

Description: Self- and semi-supervised representation learning models based on a Contrastive Learning framework to detect tics in video recordings of patients with Gilles de la Tourette Syndrome under limited labeled data, comparing several classifiers built on top of the learned representations.

Additional Information: Accepted and presented at SKILL student conference 2024

Year: 2023

Type: Bachelor Thesis

Description: Extends binary video-based tic detection to predict tic severity (score 0-7) in patients with Gilles de la Tourette Syndrome, comparing four supervised deep learning methods: multi-class classification, weighted multi-class classification, regression, and ordinal regression. Shows that video-based severity prediction is feasible, with the ordinal regression model performing best.

Additional Information: Accepted and presented at SKILL student conference 2024
